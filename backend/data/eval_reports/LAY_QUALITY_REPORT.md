## Findings (2026-11-14)

**Search (retrieval runs).** Two changes, measured separately with cached intake:

| Change | Tuning hits (of 30) | Held-out hits (of 10) |
|---|---|---|
| Before | 13 | 4 |
| Intake told the real legal areas and statute vocabulary | 18 | 3 |
| + reranker uses intake's legal terms, weighted by rarity | 22 | 3 |

- The intake change fixed the clearest failures: arrest and police questions now reach the BNSS instead of the BNS, evidence questions reach the BSA, and searches no longer use repealed CrPC / IPC / Evidence Act numbers. It also cost one held-out hit (online defamation, rank 4 before).
- **The held-out set did not improve (4 → 3).** With 10 questions one flip is within noise, but there is no evidence yet that the gains generalise beyond the tuning phrasings. All 40 together: 17 → 25.
- Vectors on vs off differ by at most one question, so Render (vectors off) gets nearly the same results.
- `product_quality` stayed at 45/45 throughout.

**Answer prompt (answer runs, gpt-oss-120b, same 10 questions, same retrieval).** The old prompt demanded 4-7 sentences, practical steps and a closing "missing fact" sentence; the new one caps at 6, allows steps only where a supplied section lays down the procedure, and drops the closing sentence (missing facts are shown separately). Refusals 4 → 1, verified sentences 14 → 20, sentences shown as unverified 23 → 6. The mean-score column is not comparable between these two rows: the old-prompt run predates the evidence-score change.

**Evidence score.** `source_relevance` compared the user's everyday words with statute text, sat at 0.10-0.40 even for fully verified answers, and triggered the 49 cap on almost every answer. It now averages that with the best match against the intake's legal framing. On the same cached runs, fully verified answers score 80-86 and partly rejected ones 51-60; no answer/refuse decision changed.

**qwen/qwen3.8-27b.** Zero refusals and 44 verified sentences look better than gpt-oss-120b, but twice it pasted statute text verbatim (copied text always passes the support check), once from a section irrelevant to the question. Added only as the fallback after gpt-oss-120b's daily quota, not as the primary.
